# Observer notes: human-style round, Accenture DSA SDE-I, 45 min, interviewer Nandini Rao (female)
Candidate played: JIIT Noida fresher, Android dev (Dunzo intern, Hisaab, RailRadar). Text-to-text, virtual clock. No voice.
Each note: [mm:ss] what happened -> why it breaks realism -> fix idea.
- [00:09] Opening "I'm a software engineer. Thanks for joining this session today" is generic and slightly stiff; a real interviewer says team/what they work on ("I'm on the payments platform team at Accenture") -> add team/role flavour to the persona opening.
- [00:31] "seeing your Hisaab app live on the Play Store already puts you ahead of most freshers" -> comparative verdict in minute 1 (also breaks the no-verdict rule and sounds flattering/fake). Fix: forbid comparisons with other candidates; allow plain interest ("I saw Hisaab is on the Play Store, nice").
- [00:31] "Ready to get started?" only 22 s in, after one nervous line: rushes past small talk. Real interviewers spend 1 to 2 exchanges (where are you from, how was the commute/day). Fix: require 2 small-talk exchanges before offering to start.
- [00:55] "Here's how the next forty-five minutes will run... two coding problems" -> promises two problems up front. In a real round you don't commit to two ("we'll see how the time goes"); and the structure lecture is long right before the first question. Fix: say "one or two problems" and keep it to one sentence.
- [01:58] "That's real work, not toy stuff." -> sounds patronising ("toy" implies the candidate's work might have been). Avoid evaluative praise of the candidate's work; ask the next question.
- [02:35] Moved to coding after 5 background exchanges (about 2.5 min) with a flat transition ("that's exactly the kind of reading that sticks. Let's move into the first coding problem"). Pace is fine for a screening round, but the hand-off should be softer ("Let's switch to some coding") and the praise dropped. The interviewer announced AND showed in the same turn, good.
- [03:28] After my 3 clarifying questions the interviewer answered 2 (ignored "very big numbers") and then said "Take a moment to read it, and tell me what constraints you'd assume before you start." -> I had just asked about constraints and had clearly read it; the line is the same restate-the-problem boilerplate as in the live round. - (harness) the "room layout -> work" line prints one turn late: workspace_news runs before the spawned room.state task; product sends it right after the turn. Fix the harness, not the product.
- [03:28] Blueprint clarifications for this problem cover only order, empty and negative numbers; "very big numbers" (range/size) has no prepared answer, so the interviewer skipped it. Fix: blueprint generation should always include a size/value-range clarification for array problems.
- [04:03] "walk me through your approach in more detail, and tell me the time and space" right after I had given approach and time for three approaches: asks for what was just said. Fix: the director's next move should name what is genuinely missing (space of the final approach) instead of a generic "in more detail".
- [04:54] Good: it saw the missing seen.add and asked for a dry run instead of telling me. Small: "Let me point at one thing" but nothing was pointed at (no code_comment/highlight) -> either point at the line or drop the phrase.

- [06:11 to 07:04] BIG: the interviewer said "Let's try a variation now, a fresh problem, since you handled that one cleanly." and then nothing: no next_item, no show_problem, and no nudge for 53 s of silence (the nudge is suppressed on a problem, and the declared-move-on backstop needs a log_turn decision that is rarely logged). - [01:42 to 02:39] BIG: before the problem was allowed or on screen, the interviewer SPOKE the problem statement three times ("Given an array of integers, determine whether any value appears at least twice.") and then said "This one's on your screen now" (blocked) and "Let me put the problem up on your screen so you can read it properly" (blocked), leaving me with three dangling stubs.
